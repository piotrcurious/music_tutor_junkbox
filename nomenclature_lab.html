Three notes form a triad. The distance from C to E is four semitones; from C to G is seven.

The small algebra

Distance becomes ratio

f(n) = f₀ × 2n/12
+4 semitones → 24/12 ≈ 1.260×

In equal temperament, each semitone multiplies frequency by the same factor. Four steps above a note is about 1.260 times its frequency.

A pocket glossary

The words, without the fog

Four ideas to keep nearby
01 / PITCH

Note, semitone, octave

A note names a pitch. A semitone is one neighbouring step on this twelve-note map. Twelve steps make an octave: the note returns, but at twice or half the frequency.

02 / SPELLING

Sharp, flat, natural

A sharp (♯) raises a note by one semitone; a flat (♭) lowers it by one. A natural (♮) cancels an earlier sharp or flat. C♯ and D♭ usually sound the same on this keyboard: two spellings, one pitch.

03 / SCALE

Major and minor

A scale is a selected set of notes with a starting point. Major follows 2–2–1–2–2–2–1; natural minor follows 2–1–2–2–1–2–2. The different spacing changes the musical character, not a fixed emotion.

04 / HARMONY

Interval, chord, key

An interval is the distance between two notes. A chord sounds several notes together. A key is a musical centre and its usual scale; its key signature tells you which notes are routinely sharpened or flattened.
