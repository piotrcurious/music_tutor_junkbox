1 · Scale generator

The first mode is the canonical logarithmic division: log₂(fᵢ/f₀) = i/N, equivalently fᵢ = f₀·2^(i/N). Every step has the same ratio and the same cents size. Equal-Hz spacing is a different geometry.

Cents-step widths form a geometric sequence across the octave. R = 1 gives equal log-frequency division; R > 1 widens intervals toward the octave; R < 1 narrows them. R is the final step divided by the first, independent of the number of subdivisions.

The lattice enumerates reduced fractions p/q within the limit, folds each ratio into one octave, and merges near-duplicates. It is a finite sample of the rational pitch lattice, not a claim that nature supplies a finite scale.

How to interpret these estimates

Select at least two notes to calculate cross-partial interactions.

Roughness uses exp(−3.5s) − exp(−5.75s), with s = partial separation / ERB at the pair’s mean frequency. Partial amplitudes are weighted by 1/nᵅ. The 0–100 index averages only partial pairs within one estimated ERB and normalizes that amplitude-weighted mean against the peak of this roughness kernel; it is comparative, not calibrated loudness or a universal consonance scale.

Beat rate is |fₐ−fᵦ|. Exact coincidences are harmonic alignment, not beating. This display includes nonzero differences up to 300 Hz only when the partials fall within one estimated ERB. The slow / flutter / roughness-region divisions are heuristic, not universal perceptual boundaries.

Second-order products are f₂±f₁. Third-order products include 2f₁±f₂ and 2f₂±f₁. Their displayed strength is an amplitude-product proxy only. Proxies are normalized separately within each order and must not be compared across orders; actual levels require a specified nonlinear transfer function, coefficient, operating level and phase.

Analysis includes cross-voice partial pairs. To keep dense chords responsive, at most eight selected voices and sixteen partials per voice are used for the intermodulation estimate; the main spectrum retains its full selected partial count.

Mathematical model and limitations
Equal divisions: f(k) = f₀ · 2^(k/N)
Harmonic ratios: c(r) = 1200 · frac(log₂(r))
Partial n: fₙ = n · f₀ · 2^(c/1200)

For the ratio lattice, p/q is reduced before filtering. A prime limit allows only prime factors no larger than the chosen limit; the numerator and denominator are each bounded by the harmonic limit. Ratios are folded modulo the octave and near-identical pitch classes are merged. Because the rational lattice is dense, increasing the limit creates more positions but does not converge to a finite “natural” scale.

The expanded chord catalogue includes triads, neutral and septimal structures, sixths, sevenths, extended chords, quartal/quintal stacks and harmonic-partial sonorities. Each preset starts from rational target ratios and snaps to the nearest available scale degrees.

Scale-derived chord discovery uses every pitch class in the active scale, ranks a bounded subset by low-complexity root ratios, then scores 3–6 voice combinations by pairwise harmonic-ratio fit. This bounded search makes dense grids tractable; it is a ranked candidate generator, not an exhaustive enumeration of every possible subset. The ratio bank is a scoring reference, while the actual chord tones always come from the selected scale.

The audio engine uses additive harmonic synthesis with amplitudes proportional to 1/nᵅ. The spectrum chart uses the same partial count, but coincidence counts are geometric diagnostics, not psychoacoustic consonance scores.

Pitch is represented logarithmically because intervals are frequency ratios. Octave folding uses modulo 1200 cents; the equal-tempered grid is one possible coordinate lattice, while the harmonic ratio scale is nonuniform.
