One useful distinction
Musical names describe organised relationships. The ear does not receive labels like “C minor”; it receives pressure changes and builds a percept from overlapping mechanical and neural evidence.

Why “C major”?Geometry → algebra

C is the root. E is four semitones above it; G is seven. The major quality is the interval pattern 0–4–7. Move the whole shape around the circle and the quality stays the same.

The clicked region suggests a centre frequency, but nearby pitches and harmonic interpretations remain possible.

Mechanical

The wave travels along the basilar membrane. Different places respond best to different frequency regions; excitation spreads beyond a single point.

Neural

Hair cells convert motion into electrical signals. Nerve fibres represent sound through population activity and timing, not one labelled neuron per note.

Perceptual

The brain infers pitch, timbre and grouping from incomplete, overlapping evidence. Context helps resolve what the spectrum alone cannot.

Model note♯ raises a note by one semitone; ♭ lowers it. In 12-tone equal temperament, C♯ and D♭ share a frequency, though their spelling depends on musical context. This is a learning model, not a physiological simulator: “memory” mode synthesises a candidate sound, not a listener’s private memory or the full neural code.
