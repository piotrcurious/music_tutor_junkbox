One useful distinction
Musical names describe organised relationships. The ear does not receive labels like “C minor”; it receives pressure changes and builds a percept from overlapping mechanical and neural evidence.

Model boundary. This is a time-domain educational reduction: Greenwood place mapping + ERB-tuned resonator channels + level-dependent active gain/compression + saturating hair-cell transduction + a low-pass receptor potential + adaptive stochastic synapse/fibre firing with refractoriness and timing jitter. It approximates travelling-wave delay and tuning, but does not solve cochlear-fluid hydrodynamics, the full organ-of-Corti micromechanics, or a biologically complete synapse.

Scientific basis and model assumptions

Place map: Greenwood's human frequency–position function, CF(x) = 165.4 × (102.1x − 0.88) Hz, with x normalised from apex (0) to base (1). Auditory filters: equivalent rectangular bandwidth approximation, ERB = 24.7 × (4.37 CF[kHz] + 1); Q is derived from CF / (1.019 × ERB). Active mechanics: a 12 ms local envelope controller reduces OHC gain as drive rises; this is a phenomenological compressive controller, not a measured cochlear feedback law. IHC: MET uses a sigmoid with 15% resting open probability and a 0.02 model-unit slope; receptor-potential low-pass time constants vary from 0.35–1.45 ms with CF. Synapse: each fibre has a resource state R with recovery τ = 75 ms and drive-dependent depletion, using D = [d/(d + 0.05)]² and dR/dt = (1 − R)/0.075 − 0.6DR/0.08. Spikes: inhomogeneous Poisson events with 0.8 ms absolute and 4 ms relative refractoriness, plus CF-scaled timing jitter. These constants are illustrative model parameters, not universal human measurements. The three fibre classes are teaching archetypes, not a fitted population. In progression mode, resonator states, IHC potentials, synaptic resources and refractory histories persist across chord changes.

Mechanical

A fluid-driven travelling wave grows as it moves from base toward its characteristic place, then decays. Place is tonotopic; tuning is finite, level-dependent and overlapping.

Neural

Inner hair-cell mechanoelectrical transduction is graded and saturating. Ribbon synapses drive heterogeneous fibres; spikes carry place, rate and (at low frequencies) temporal/phase-locked information.

Perceptual

The brain infers pitch, timbre and grouping from incomplete, overlapping evidence. Context helps resolve what the spectrum alone cannot.

Model note♯ raises a note by one semitone; ♭ lowers it. In 12-tone equal temperament, C♯ and D♭ share a frequency, though their spelling depends on musical context. The cochlear display is a reduced-order educational simulation, not a full fluid-mechanical or cellular model. “Memory” mode synthesises candidate sounds from ambiguous cues; it cannot access a listener’s private memory.
